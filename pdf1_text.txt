1. Literature Review (2020–2026)
================================================================================

Foundational RAG (2020). Lewis et al. (2020, NeurIPS) introduced retrieval-augmented
generation for knowledge-intensive NLP tasks — the base architecture (retriever +
generator) nearly all later clinical-RAG work extends.

Hallucination as the core motivating problem (2023–2024). Clinical-LLM literature is
unusually explicit about the stakes: prior studies reported hallucination rates of
28.6–39.6% in medical-specialized models without retrieval grounding. This is the
direct justification for why RAG (not a bare LLM) is the right architecture for
clinical decision support.

Domain-grounded RAG systems (2024). Guideline-grounded systems emerged across
specialties (hepatology, ophthalmology, radiology) — consistently showing that
narrow, specialty-specific grounding reduces hallucination substantially, but each
system was built and evaluated in isolation with inconsistent methodology.

Architecture sophistication (2024–2025). Hybrid RAG combining dense and sparse
retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion, and knowledge-graph-enhanced retrieval over
UMLS (a ~3M-node clinical concept graph) represent the current state of the art.
Privacy-preserving, on-premises deployment variants ("MediRAG") address
institutional data-residency requirements.

Quantified hallucination-reduction results (2025). A radiology contrast-media
consultation study found complete elimination of hallucinations (reducing incidence
from 8% to 0%) when using a RAG-enhanced local LLM compared to the ungrounded
baseline — a strong, citable, concrete result for evaluation framing.

RAG vs. fine-tuning debate (2025). A systematic review concluded RAG is generally the
more resource-efficient and scalable approach for medical LLM deployment, since
fine-tuning requires substantial compute and annotated data often unavailable in
clinical settings.

Evaluation methodology remains the weak point (2024–2026). Work on "Ranking Over
Scoring" (COLING 2025) argues for more reliable automated evaluation of LLM-
generated medical explanations. Across nearly every paper reviewed, hallucination-
rate measurement methodology differs (human review vs. automated fact-checking
vs. LLM-as-judge), making cross-paper comparison unreliable.

Summary Table
  Period     | Focus                    | Key contribution
  -----------|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------
  2020       | Foundational architecture | RAG introduced for knowledge-intensive NLP
  2023–2024  | Problem motivation        | Hallucination rates 28.6–39.6% documented
  2024       | Domain-grounded systems   | Specialty-specific guideline-grounded RAG
  2024–2025  | Architecture              | Hybrid dense+sparse; UMLS knowledge-graph RAG
  2025       | Quantified results        | RAG reduces hallucination 8% → 0% (radiology)
  2025       | RAG vs fine-tuning        | RAG favored for resource-constrained settings
  2024–2026  | Evaluation gap            | No standardized hallucination-measurement methodology

2. Research Gap Analysis
================================================================================

1. No standardized, reproducible hallucination-evaluation protocol. Every paper
   measures hallucination differently — this is explicitly flagged across multiple
   2024–2026 sources.

2. Confidence-calibrated abstention is underexplored. Most systems always answer;
   very few explicitly detect low-retrieval-confidence situations and abstain or flag
   uncertainty — a real patient-safety gap, since a confidently wrong answer is more
   dangerous than "I'm not sure, consult a specialist."

3. Narrow, single-specialty focus limits generalizable conclusions. Existing systems
   are each built and evaluated in isolation.

Novelty claim: a guideline-grounded RAG assistant with confidence-calibrated
abstention (Adaptive Evidence Budgeting), evaluated using a rigorous, reproducible
hallucination-measurement protocol combining RAGAS automated fact-checking
and structured human review — directly addressing the methodology-inconsistency
gap while adding a genuine safety mechanism most existing systems lack.

3. Existing Solutions Comparison
================================================================================

  System/Approach                      | Strength                           | Weakness (our opening)
  -------------------------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------
  Vanilla LLM (no retrieval)           | Simple, fast                       | 28.6–39.6% hallucination rate — unsafe
  Hepatology/Ophthalmology RAG (2024)  | Strong specialty hallucination cut  | Narrow scope, no abstention mechanism
  Hybrid dense+sparse + UMLS KG RAG   | State-of-the-art retrieval          | Too complex for 3–4 month timeline
  MediRAG (privacy-preserving)         | Addresses data-residency            | Privacy focus, not abstention/eval rigor
  Fine-tuned medical LLM (no RAG)     | Strong on narrow diagnostic tasks   | Needs substantial compute/annotated data

Recommendation implemented: Advanced RAG (hybrid + rerank) with a
corrective/abstention (AEB) layer, using ChromaDB + BGE-M3, no knowledge
graph (not our novelty axis and would eat our timeline).

4. Novel Contribution
================================================================================

"Confidence-Calibrated Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Safe Clinical
Decision Support via Adaptive Evidence Budgeting"

The AEB loop iteratively retrieves document batches (initial_k=3, step_k=3,
max_k=15) until confidence ≥ θ, then abstains rather than hallucinating when
the corpus cannot ground the answer. The confidence score is a multi-signal
fusion: retriever sufficiency + evidence agreement + hallucination penalty
(DeBERTa-v3-MNLI NLI check), calibrated via temperature scaling.

This is evaluated via:
  (a) RAGAS automated metrics (Faithfulness, Answer Relevancy, Context
      Precision, Context Recall) — directly citable against published RAG papers
  (b) Custom abstention-rate curve: sweep θ ∈ {0.5, 0.6, ..., 0.9} and plot
      the precision/coverage Pareto curve — this is the key novel result figure

                      context_recall, run_at

  Collection: guideline_chunks — stores embeddings + metadata per chunk

11. AI/ML Model Design (Updated — Resolved)
================================================================================

Retriever:
  BGE-M3 (FlagEmbedding) — hybrid-native (dense + sparse + multi-vector
  in one model). MIT license. Free, self-hostable.

NLI Verifier:
  DeBERTa-v3-large-MNLI — claim-level entailment checking.
  Threshold: entailment probability > 0.7.

Generation LLM — three serving backends, auto-selected by detect_hardware():

  Backend           | Hardware            | Quantization | max_new_tokens
  ------------------|---------------------|--------------|---------------
  local_4bit        | RTX 4050 (6 GB)     | 4-bit NF4    | 256
  kaggle_fp16       | Kaggle T4 (16 GB)   | fp16         | 512
  hf_inference_api  | HF managed (cloud)  | managed      | 256

  Hardware auto-config table:
    ≥ 20 GB VRAM → kaggle_fp16
    10–19 GB     → local_4bit (fp16-capable but conservative)
    < 10 GB      → local_4bit + RAGAS via hf_inference_api
    No GPU       → hf_inference_api for all calls

RAGAS Judge:
  Same model (Llama3-Med-8B-Instruct) via HuggingFace Inference API.
  Uses LangchainLLMWrapper + HuggingFaceEndpoint.
  Free tier: ~1000 requests/day — sufficient for 200-sample MedQA eval.
  Zero additional VRAM cost.

Confidence Scorer:
  Multi-signal fusion:
    confidence = w1 * retriever_sufficiency
               + w2 * evidence_agreement
               - w3 * hallucination_penalty
  Calibrated via temperature scaling. ECE measured on validation set.

Abstention threshold (θ):
  Treated as an experimental variable. Report the precision/coverage
  tradeoff curve (θ sweep ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.75, 0.8, 0.85, 0.9})
  not a single fixed number. This Pareto curve is Figure 1 in the paper.

13. Evaluation Metrics (Resolved Protocol)
================================================================================

RAGAS metrics (via HF Inference API judge):
  • Faithfulness — are claims grounded in retrieved chunks?
  • Answer Relevancy — does the answer address the query?
  • Context Precision — are retrieved chunks actually relevant?
  • Context Recall — were all relevant chunks retrieved?

Custom metrics:
  • Hallucination rate: % of answered responses with ≥1 unsupported claim
    (DeBERTa NLI verifier + spot human review)
  • Abstention rate: % of queries where system declines to answer
    (reported as Pareto curve with accuracy, not a single number)
  • Citation accuracy: % of cited sources that genuinely support the claim
  • Abstention-accuracy Pareto curve: sweep θ, plot (accuracy, abstention_rate,
    hallucination_rate) — this is the key novel result figure

Baselines:
  1. Vanilla LLM (no retrieval)
  2. Vanilla RAG (fixed top-5)
  3. Hybrid RAG (dense + BM25 + RRF, no abstention)
  4. AEB / Full System (ours)

18. Interview Questions Prep
================================================================================

1. Why RAG instead of fine-tuning for this use case?
2. How does your AEB loop decide when to stop retrieving vs. abstain?
3. Walk through your NLI-based hallucination verifier — where does it fail?
4. What's the difference between retrieval-similarity confidence and
   multi-signal fusion — why combine both?
5. How did you validate that your RAGAS/automated hallucination metric
   is trustworthy (inter-rater agreement with human review)?
6. What happens when the guideline corpus itself is outdated or wrong?
7. How would you handle multi-turn clinical conversations?
8. What are the real deployment blockers between this prototype and actual
   clinical use (regulatory, liability, EHR integration)?
9. Why Kaggle T4 instead of a local GPU for paper experiments?
   (Answer: RTX 4050 is 6 GB — below minimum for fp16 8B models;
    Kaggle provides 16 GB T4 for free, 30h/week.)
10. Why the same model for generation and RAGAS judging?
    (Answer: reuse loaded weights — zero extra VRAM cost on 6 GB.)
